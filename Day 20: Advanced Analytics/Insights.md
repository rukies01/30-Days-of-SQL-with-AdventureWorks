Key Lessons Learned
1. Event-level detail is critical for behavioral analysis

One of the biggest lessons today was understanding that some analyses require preserving transaction-level detail.

For example:

calculating time between purchases cannot be done after aggregation.

Using:

MAX(OrderDate)

too early removes the purchase sequence needed for timeline analysis.

Instead, each purchase event must remain as its own row.

2. LAG() compares sequential events

I learned that LAG() is useful for accessing the previous event within an entity’s timeline.

For customer purchase analysis:

LAG(OrderDate)
OVER (
    PARTITION BY CustomerKey
    ORDER BY OrderDate
)

allows SQL to compare:

current purchase
previous purchase

for the same customer.

This makes it possible to calculate purchase intervals using DATEDIFF().

3. Partitioning depends on the business entity

A major lesson was learning to ask:

“What entity owns the sequence?”

For purchase interval analysis:

the entity is the customer.

That is why:

PARTITION BY CustomerKey

is essential.

Without partitioning, SQL would incorrectly compare purchases across different customers.

4. Customer Lifetime Value (CLV) is revenue-based

I initially confused:

customer lifespan
with
customer lifetime value.

Using:

DATEDIFF(FirstPurchaseDate, LastPurchaseDate)

measures customer tenure or lifespan.

But CLV approximation is better represented by:

SUM(SalesAmount)

which measures the total revenue generated by each customer.

5. Different customer metrics answer different business questions

Today also showed that customer analytics can be measured in multiple ways:

Metric	Business Meaning
Recency	How recently a customer purchased
Frequency	How often they purchase
Monetary	How much revenue they generate
Purchase Interval	Time gap between purchases

These concepts are foundational in customer analytics and retention analysis.

6. Window functions preserve detail while adding context

Functions like:

LAG()
SUM() OVER()
MAX() OVER()

allow calculations across rows without collapsing the dataset.

This makes them powerful for:

customer journey analysis,
trend analysis,
retention analysis,
and advanced business reporting.
